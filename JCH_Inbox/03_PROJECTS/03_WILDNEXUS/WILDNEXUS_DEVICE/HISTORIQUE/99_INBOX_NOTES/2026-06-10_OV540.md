For a battery-powered wildlife setup capturing movement between 3 to 12 meters, configuring a fixed-focus OV5640with an ESP32-S3 requires careful lens selection and strict low-power battery management.
1. The Right Lens Configuration (3 to 12 Meters)
Standard stock lenses on cheap OV5640 modules usually feature an ultra-wide 120° to 170° fisheye lens. At a distance of 12 meters, a medium-sized animal (like a deer or fox) will only occupy a few tiny, unidentifiable pixels in an ultra-wide frame. [1] 

The Recommendation: Select an Arducam module featuring an M12 lens holder paired with a 6mm or 8mm narrow-angle lens (roughly a 40° to 50° Horizontal Field of View).
The Reason: This slightly narrow lens naturally acts as a optical zoom. It compresses the depth-of-field, ensuring an animal standing 12 meters away fills enough of the 5MP frame to be clearly captured.
Focus Setting: Manually thread the M12 lens into the holder until the focus is sharp at roughly 8 meters (the midpoint of your range). Tighten the lock ring or use a drop of glue to freeze it. Because it is fixed-focus, the depth of field will keep everything from 3 meters out to infinity perfectly crisp.
2. Battery Optimization Strategies
An active ESP32-S3 and an OV5640 sensor will completely drain a standard 18650 lithium battery in less than a day if left running. [2, 3] 

Hardware Power-Gating: Ensure your ESP32-S3 development board features a MOSFET switch that can completely cut the power line (VCC/DOVDD) to the OV5640 sensor while sleeping. The OV5640 has a known hardware quirk where its internal voltage regulator draws residual power even when the sensor is via code in software standby. [2, 4] 
Deep Sleep & External PIR Trigger: Use an external Passive Infrared (PIR) motion sensor wired to an RTC GPIO pin on the ESP32-S3. Program the ESP32-S3 to stay in a deep sleep mode (drawing microamps). When an animal triggers the PIR sensor, it will instantly wake the ESP32-S3, power up the camera, snapshot the image to the MicroSD card, and drop straight back to sleep. [2, 3, 5, 6, 7]

1. Hardware Architecture & Signal Routing
Because you are recording video data to an SD card, you must use 4-bit SDMMC mode on the ESP32-S3 rather than slow SPI mode. [3] 

The Reason: SPI mode is too slow to handle the continuous stream of MJPEG frames coming from the OV5640. If the SD card write speed bottlenecks, your camera buffer will overflow, causing dropped frames or crashing. [4] 
Pin Allocation Caution: The 24-pin DVP parallel interface for the OV5640 requires roughly 11 to 15 GPIO pins. 4-bit SDMMC requires another 6 pins. Ensure your chosen development board (such as the Waveshare ESP32-S3 Cam or an Arducam ESP32-S3 package) natively routes the SD card to the dedicated SDMMC peripheral slots to avoid GPIO resource conflict. [4, 5] 
2. The Notification Pipeline (Low Data Overhead)
Since you are not sending large video files over the air, you only need to establish a momentary Wi-Fi connection. The lightest methods for sending a notification from an ESP32-S3 include:

Telegram Bot API: By far the most reliable, free method. The ESP32-S3 wakes up, connects to Wi-Fi, and executes a tiny HTTP POST request to the Telegram Bot API. You receive an instant push notification on your phone stating: "Movement Detected: Video_042.avi saved safely to SD card." [6, 7, 8, 9, 10] 
Twilio API (SMS / WhatsApp): If you prefer standard cellular text alerts or WhatsApp notifications, use the Twilio API Client. The text string uses minimal bytes of data. [11, 12] 
MQTT Pub/Sub: If you integrate this into a wider smart home ecosystem (like Home Assistant), you can publish a basic "wildlife/triggered" = "true" topic string.
