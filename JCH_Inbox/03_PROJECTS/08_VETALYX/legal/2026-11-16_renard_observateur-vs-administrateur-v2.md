## PARTIE A — RÉPONSE EN ANGLAIS (prête à envoyer)

Dear colleagues,

Thank you for your questions. Below is an objective overview of the two roles under Belgian company law (the Belgian Code of Companies and Associations — "CSA", 2019), which will govern our future SRL (société à responsabilité limitée). We have prepared this so we can decide together on the most appropriate governance structure.

### 1. Company director (administrateur) in Belgium

In a Belgian SRL, the company is managed by one or more **directors** (administrateurs), who may be natural persons or legal persons. The directors form the board (conseil d'administration).

**Appointment and term.** Directors are appointed by the shareholders' meeting. In an SRL the term may be for a definite period (in principle up to six years, renewable) or, if the articles so provide, for an indefinite period (CSA Art. 5:71).

**Powers.** The board holds the **residual management power**: it may take every decision and perform every act useful or necessary to realise the corporate purpose, except those the law or the articles reserve to the shareholders (CSA Art. 5:70). The board also represents the company towards third parties, subject to the allocation of powers in the articles.

**Duties and obligations.** A director owes the company:
- a duty of **care, diligence and prudence**;
- a duty of **loyalty** — to act in the **corporate interest**, not for a personal or third-party interest;
- a duty of **confidentiality**;
- the duty to **account** for the management.

A specific and important obligation is the **conflict-of-interest regime** (CSA Art. 5:76). Whenever a director has a direct or indirect financial interest that conflicts with an interest of the company, the director must declare it, abstain from deliberating and voting on that item, and a specific procedure must be followed (declaration, minutes, mention in the annual report). Non-compliance can lead to liability and, in some cases, to the annulment of the decision.

**Personal liability.** Directors are **personally liable** for management faults (CSA Art. 5:98) and, under specific regimes, for tax and social-security debts and for wrongfully continuing an insolvent activity (CSA Art. 5:99–5:101). In practice, liability attaches to a **manifestly faulty** decision (the "business judgment rule"): an honest business decision is not sanctioned; a clearly unreasonable one is.

**A point specific to legal persons as directors.** Where a legal person is appointed director, the CSA requires it to appoint a **natural person as its "permanent representative"** (représentant permanent), who exercises the mandate in the legal person's name and on its behalf and who bears the **same civil and criminal liability** as if acting personally, jointly and severally with the legal person (CSA Art. 2:55). The same article prohibits a "double capacity": the permanent representative **may not also sit on that board in a personal capacity, or as the representative of another legal-person director**. This rule is directly relevant to our situation and is one of the reasons we favour the observer route (see section 4 below).

In short: a director **decides, manages, represents the company, and bears personal responsibility**, including a formal conflict-of-interest procedure and, for a legal person, the permanent-representative machinery.

### 2. Observer (observateur) in Belgium

An important point of legal certainty: **the Belgian CSA does not define or regulate an "observer" (observateur).** The observer is **not a statutory office**. It is a purely **contractual** arrangement, created by the shareholders in the shareholders' agreement (pacte d'actionnaires) and/or in the board's rules of procedure (règlement d'ordre intérieur).

Because it is contractual, its content is defined freely and precisely by the parties. An observer is typically granted:

- the right to **attend board meetings** (in whole or in part, as agreed);
- the right to **receive information and documents** (agenda, minutes, financial information), subject to a confidentiality undertaking and to the company's legitimate interests;
- the right to **express views, ask questions and submit observations**;
- the right to have those observations **recorded in the minutes**.

An observer is **not** granted, by the very nature of the role:

- **no voting right** (the observer does not deliberate);
- **no management power** and **no power to bind the company**;
- **no director's mandate** — and therefore **no director liability** (Art. 5:98–5:101 do not apply);
- **no conflict-of-interest procedure** (Art. 5:76 applies to directors only);
- **no permanent-representative requirement** (Art. 2:55 applies to legal-person directors only).

This last point is decisive for our situation. A person who is both a shareholder (or the representative of a shareholder) and an observer can attend the board, be fully informed and express a view **without** triggering the conflict-of-interest obligations, **without** assuming the personal liability of a director, and **without** the double-capacity prohibition of Art. 2:55.

### 3. Comparative summary — what an observer may and may not do

| Dimension | Director (administrateur) | Observer (observateur) |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | Statutory (CSA Art. 5:70 ff.) | Contractual only (pacte / rules of procedure) |
| Attend board meetings | Yes (member of the board) | Yes (by contractual right) |
| Speak, ask questions, express views | Yes | Yes |
| Receive information | Yes | Yes (to the extent agreed) |
| Vote on decisions | Yes | **No** |
| Manage the company | Yes (residual power) | **No** |
| Represent / bind the company | Yes | **No** |
| Corporate duties (care, loyalty) | Yes | No (only good faith + confidentiality) |
| Conflict-of-interest procedure (Art. 5:76) | Applies | **Does not apply** |
| Permanent representative (Art. 2:55, legal person) | Required | **Not required** |
| Personal liability (Art. 5:98–5:101) | Yes | **No** (no mandate) |

The observer is therefore **kept informed and heard, but does not decide**. This is precisely what preserves the legal certainty of the role: participation without liability.

### 4. Why the observer route is particularly appropriate for our structure

Two concrete points follow from the above and concern our specific situation:

1. **The double-capacity prohibition.** Under CSA Art. 2:55, if Diaho (a legal person) sat on the board as a director, it would have to appoint a natural person as its permanent representative; and that representative could **not** simultaneously sit on the board in a personal capacity (for example as a founder-director). As an observer, Diaho may simply send a representative of its choice to attend and be heard, without this statutory restriction and without the permanent-representative liability regime.

2. **Diaho's shareholder position is unaffected.** The choice between director and observer concerns only **board-level participation**. Diaho's rights as a 40% shareholder — including the statutory voting rights and the thresholds set out in the articles — remain fully intact. The observer role does not reduce Diaho's control at the shareholders' level; it simply structures Diaho's participation at the board level in a way that is legally simpler and safer.

### 5. May an observer formally object to a board decision?

We answer this with complete transparency.

An observer has **no deliberative vote**. Consequently, a written objection from the observer:

- does **not suspend, block or annul** the board's decision — the decision remains valid and effective;
- is **not a veto**.

However, a written, dated and traceable objection (sent by email with proof of delivery, or by registered mail) has a real and useful **evidentiary value**:

1. it **proves that the observer did not approve** the decision and prevents any argument of tacit approval or acquiescence;
2. it **preserves the observer's position** and any rights that may follow (in particular if the observer is also a shareholder, it can support a later action at the shareholders' level);
3. it can be **recorded in the minutes** of the meeting, so that it becomes part of the company's official record;
4. in a later dispute, it constitutes contemporaneous written evidence of the disagreement.

To give it the strongest effect, the objection should be: (i) expressed at the meeting itself and minuted, (ii) dated and signed, (iii) sent through a traceable channel with proof of receipt, and (iv) linked to a concrete right (an information right, or a shareholder's right to request a shareholders' meeting).

**In summary:** the observer's written objection has strong **evidentiary and protective** value — it documents the disagreement and protects the observer's position — but it does **not** by itself prevent the decision. A genuine power to block a decision would require either a contractual veto right (which we are deliberately avoiding, to keep the structure simple and free of the director conflict-of-interest machinery) or a shareholders'-level right (such as a blocking threshold in the articles).

We hope this clarifies the two roles. We believe the observer role offers the best balance: Diaho remains fully informed and able to express its position at every board meeting, while the company and the individuals involved avoid the conflict-of-interest procedures, the permanent-representative machinery and the personal liability attached to a director's mandate. Our Belgian notary remains available to confirm these points.

## PARTIE B — SYNTHÈSE POUR JC (français)

### Les 4 points juridiques à retenir avant d'envoyer

1. **L'« observateur » n'existe pas dans le CSA.** Ce n'est pas une fonction légale. C'est une création **purement contractuelle** (pacte d'actionnaires et/ou règlement d'ordre intérieur). Conséquence : tout son contenu se définit librement — et, surtout, il n'a **ni mandat d'administrateur, ni responsabilité personnelle, ni régime de conflit d'intérêts (Art. 5:76 CSA)**. C'est l'argument central de sécurité juridique face au cumul de Peiwen QI.

2. **Art. 2:55 CSA — le point nouveau et décisif.** Si Diaho (personne morale) siégeait comme administrateur, elle devrait nommer un **représentant permanent** (personne physique) qui assume la même responsabilité civile et pénale que s'il agissait à titre personnel, solidairement avec Diaho. Et surtout, Art. 2:55 **interdit la « double casquette »** : ce représentant permanent **ne peut pas siéger en même temps à titre personnel** sur le même organe. Or Peiwen QI est déjà administratrice fondatrice à titre personnel — la config « Diaho administrateur représenté par Peiwen QI » est donc **bloquée par la loi**, pas seulement inopportune. L'observateur contourne toute cette machinerie : Diaho envoie qui elle veut, sans représentant permanent ni responsabilité associée.

3. **Le courrier de désaccord a une valeur probatoire, pas bloquante.** Un courrier écrit, daté et traçable **ne suspend ni n'annule** la décision (l'observateur n'a pas de voix délibérative, donc pas de véto). MAIS il a une **valeur probatoire réelle** : il prouve l'absence d'approbation, écarte tout acquiescement tacite, préserve la position, peut être consigné au PV et servir de preuve en contentieux. Pour le renforcer : consignation au PV, datation/signature, preuve d'envoi/réception, rattachement à un droit concret.

4. **Diaho garde ses droits d'actionnaire à 40 %.** Le passage administrateur → observateur ne touche que la **participation au conseil**. Les prérogatives d'actionnaire (majorités, seuils de blocage, AGE/AGO) restent intactes. À marteler côté chinois pour qu'ils ne perçoivent pas l'observateur comme une perte de contrôle.

### Risques et limites à surveiller

- **Tout repose sur la qualité de rédaction du pacte.** L'observateur n'étant pas statutaire, il faut figer précisément : qui nomme l'observateur, durée, réunions suivies, information reçue, confidentialité, caractère **non délibérant**.
- **Risque d'« administrateur de fait ».** Si l'observateur co-décide en pratique, un juge peut le requalifier en administrateur de fait et lui attacher la responsabilité d'un administrateur. Le rôle doit rester strictement **information + avis**.
- **Gestion des attentes.** L'observateur est informé et entendu, il ne décide pas. Sans véto, le partenaire peut se sentir dépossédé — d'où l'importance du point 4 et d'un ton rassurant mais précis.

### À faire valider par le notaire

- La formulation exacte de la clause « observateur » (droits, durée, confidentialité, caractère non délibérant, exclusion explicite de tout mandat).
- La cohérence avec l'Art. 33 des statuts (liste des administrateurs) — retirer Peiwen QI de la fonction d'administratrice si l'on adopte l'observateur, tout en conservant sa qualité de représentante de Diaho, et confirmer qu'aucun représentant permanent (Art. 2:55) n'est requis pour un observateur.
- Confirmer qu'aucun régime de conflit d'intérêts ne s'applique à l'observateur.
