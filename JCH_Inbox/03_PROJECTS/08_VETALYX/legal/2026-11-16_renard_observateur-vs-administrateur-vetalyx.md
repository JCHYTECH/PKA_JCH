## PARTIE A — RÉPONSE EN ANGLAIS (prête à envoyer)

Dear colleagues,

Thank you for your questions. Below is an objective overview of the two roles under Belgian company law (the Belgian Code of Companies and Associations — "CSA", 2019), which governs our future SRL ("société à responsabilité limitée"). We have prepared this so we can discuss the most appropriate governance structure together.

### 1. Company director (administrateur) in Belgium

In a Belgian SRL, the company is managed by one or more **directors** (administrateurs). The directors collectively form the board (conseil d'administration).

**Appointment and term.** Directors are appointed by the shareholders' meeting. In an SRL, the term may be for a definite period (in principle up to six years, renewable) or, if the articles so provide, for an indefinite period (CSA Art. 5:71).

**Powers.** The board holds the **residual management power** (pouvoir résiduel de gestion): it may take every decision and perform every act that is useful or necessary to realise the company's corporate purpose, except those that the law or the articles expressly reserve to the shareholders (CSA Art. 5:70). The board also represents the company towards third parties (power of representation), subject to the allocation of powers set out in the articles.

**Duties and obligations.** A director owes the company:
- a duty of **care, diligence and prudence** (acting as a reasonably careful manager);
- a duty of **loyalty**, i.e. to act in the **corporate interest** and not for a personal or third-party interest;
- a duty of **confidentiality** regarding information received in that capacity;
- the duty to **account** for the management.

A specific and important obligation is the **conflict-of-interest regime** (CSA Art. 5:76). Whenever a director has a direct or indirect financial interest that conflicts with an interest of the company, that director must disclose it, abstain from deliberating and voting on that item, and a specific procedure must be followed (declaration, minutes, mention in the annual report). Non-compliance can lead to liability and, in certain cases, to the annulment of the decision.

**Personal liability.** Directors are **personally liable** for management faults (CSA Art. 5:98), and under specific regimes for tax and social security debts and for wrongfully continuing an insolvent activity (CSA Art. 5:99–5:101). In practice, liability attaches to a **manifestly faulty** decision (the margin known as the "business judgment rule"): an honest business decision is not sanctioned, but a clearly unreasonable one is.

In short: a director **decides, manages, represents the company, and bears personal responsibility** for doing so, including a formal conflict-of-interest procedure whenever a personal interest is involved.

### 2. Observer (observateur) in Belgium

An important point of legal certainty: **the Belgian CSA does not define or regulate the role of an "observer".** The observer is **not a statutory office**. It is a purely **contractual** arrangement, created by the shareholders in the shareholders' agreement (pacte d'actionnaires) and/or in the board's rules of procedure (règlement d'ordre intérieur).

Because it is contractual, its content can be defined freely and precisely by the parties. An observer is typically granted:

- the right to **attend board meetings** (in whole or in part, as agreed);
- the right to **receive information and documents** (agenda, minutes, financial information), subject to a confidentiality undertaking and to the company's legitimate interests;
- the right to **express views, ask questions and submit observations**;
- the right to have those observations **recorded in the minutes** of the meeting.

An observer is **not** granted, by the very nature of the role:

- **no voting right** (the observer does not deliberate);
- **no management power** and **no power to bind the company**;
- **no director's mandate** — and therefore **no director liability** (the regimes of Art. 5:98–5:101 do not apply);
- **no conflict-of-interest procedure** (the regime of Art. 5:76 applies to directors only).

This last point is decisive for our situation. A person who is both a shareholder (or the representative of a shareholder) and an observer can attend the board, be fully informed and express a view **without** triggering the conflict-of-interest obligations and **without** assuming the personal liability of a director.

### 3. Comparative summary — what an observer may and may not do

| Dimension | Director (administrateur) | Observer (observateur) |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | Statutory (CSA Art. 5:70 ff.) | Contractual only (pacte / rules of procedure) |
| Attend board meetings | Yes (member of the board) | Yes (by contractual right) |
| Speak, ask questions, express views | Yes | Yes |
| Receive information | Yes | Yes (to the extent agreed) |
| Vote on decisions | Yes | **No** |
| Manage the company | Yes (residual power) | **No** |
| Represent / bind the company | Yes | **No** |
| Corporate duties (care, loyalty) | Yes | No (only good faith + confidentiality) |
| Conflict-of-interest procedure (Art. 5:76) | Applies | **Does not apply** |
| Personal liability (Art. 5:98–5:101) | Yes | **No** (no mandate) |

The observer is therefore **kept informed and heard, but does not decide**. This is exactly what preserves the legal certainty of the role: participation without liability.

### 4. May an observer formally object to a board decision?

This is a natural question, and we answer it with complete transparency.

An observer has **no deliberative vote**. Consequently, a written objection from the observer:

- does **not suspend, block or annul** the board's decision — the decision remains valid and effective;
- is **not a veto**.

However, a written, dated and traceable objection (sent by email with proof of delivery, or by registered mail) has a real and useful **evidentiary value**:

1. it **proves that the observer did not approve** the decision and prevents any argument of tacit approval or acquiescence;
2. it **preserves the observer's position** and any rights that may follow (in particular if the observer is also a shareholder, it can support a later action at the level of the shareholders);
3. it can be **recorded in the minutes** of the meeting, so that it becomes part of the company's official record;
4. in a later dispute, it constitutes contemporaneous written evidence of the disagreement.

To give it the strongest effect, the objection should be: (i) expressed at the meeting itself and minuted, (ii) dated and signed, (iii) sent through a traceable channel with proof of receipt, and (iv) linked to a concrete right (an information right, or a shareholder's right to request a shareholders' meeting).

**In summary:** the observer's written objection has strong **evidentiary and protective** value — it documents the disagreement and protects the observer's position — but it does **not** by itself prevent the decision. A genuine power to block a decision would require either a contractual veto right (which we are deliberately avoiding, to keep the structure simple and free of the director conflict-of-interest machinery) or a shareholders'-level right (such as a blocking threshold in the articles).

We hope this clarifies the two roles. We believe the observer role offers the best balance: Diaho remains fully informed and able to express its position at every board meeting, while the company and the individuals involved avoid the conflict-of-interest procedures and the personal liability attached to a director's mandate. We are happy to discuss any aspect, and our Belgian notary remains available to confirm these points.

## PARTIE B — SYNTHÈSE POUR JC (français)

### Les 3 points juridiques à retenir avant d'envoyer

1. **L'« observateur » n'existe pas dans le CSA.** Ce n'est pas une fonction légale. C'est une création **purement contractuelle** (pacte d'actionnaires et/ou règlement d'ordre intérieur du conseil). Conséquence : tout son contenu se définit librement — et, surtout, il n'a **ni mandat d'administrateur, ni responsabilité personnelle, ni régime de conflit d'intérêts (Art. 5:76 CSA)**. C'est l'argument central de sécurité juridique face au cumul de Peiwen QI (fondatrice 15 % + représentante Diaho 40 %).

2. **Le courrier de désaccord a une valeur probatoire, pas bloquante.** Réponse honnête à ta question : un courrier écrit, daté et traçable (email avec preuve de réception, ou recommandé) **ne suspend ni n'annule** la décision — l'observateur n'a pas de voix délibérative, donc pas de véto. MAIS il a une **valeur probatoire réelle** : il prouve l'absence d'approbation, écarte tout acquiescement tacite, préserve la position de l'observateur et peut nourrir le PV ou un contentieux ultérieur. Pour le renforcer : le faire consigner au PV de la réunion, le dater/signer, garder la preuve d'envoi/réception, et le rattacher à un droit concret (droit d'information, ou droit d'actionnaire si l'observateur est aussi actionnaire).

3. **Diaho garde ses droits d'actionnaire à 40 % quoi qu'il arrive.** Le passage administrateur → observateur ne touche que la **participation au conseil**. Les prérogatives d'actionnaire (majorités statutaires, seuils de blocage, droits à l'AGE/AGO) restent intactes. C'est le point à marteler côté chinois pour qu'ils ne perçoivent pas l'observateur comme une perte de contrôle.

### Risques et limites à surveiller

- **Tout repose sur la qualité de rédaction du pacte.** L'observateur n'étant pas statutaire, si le pacte est silencieux ou mal rédigé, ses droits sont faibles. Il faut fixer précisément : qui nomme l'observateur, pour quelle durée, quelles réunions il suit, quelle information il reçoit, la confidentialité, et le caractère **non délibérant**.
- **Risque d'« administrateur de fait ».** Si l'observateur participe de fait à la gestion au point de co-décider, un juge belge peut le requalifier en administrateur de fait et lui attacher la responsabilité d'un administrateur, malgré l'étiquette. Le rôle doit rester strictement **information + avis**, sans vote ni gestion.
- **Gestion des attentes.** L'observateur est informé et entendu, il ne décide pas. Sans véto, le partenaire peut se sentir dépossédé — d'où l'importance du point 3 ci-dessus et d'un ton rassurant mais précis dans la réponse EN.

### À faire valider par le notaire

- La formulation exacte de la clause « observateur » dans le pacte (droits, durée, confidentialité, caractère non délibérant, exclusion explicite de tout mandat).
- La cohérence avec l'Art. 33 des statuts (liste des administrateurs) si l'on retire Peiwen QI de la fonction d'administratrice tout en conservant sa qualité de représentante de Diaho.
- La confirmation qu'aucun régime de conflit d'intérêts statutaire ne s'applique à l'observateur.
