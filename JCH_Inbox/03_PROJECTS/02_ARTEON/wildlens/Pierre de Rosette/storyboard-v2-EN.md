# Storyboard V2 — "Why We Still Can't Talk to Animals"

> WildLens video essay · **~5 min** · VO EN · YouTube
> **Key:** REEL = real photos/video · IA = AI abstract clip (never generated animals) · MOTION = animated diagram/type
> **Tone:** simple, spoken, didactic — like a conversation, not a lecture

---

## Scene 1 — The Dream (0:00 → 0:40)

**VO:**
You've dreamed of it too, haven't you? Talking to animals. Knowing what your dog really means. Understanding why the bird sings at dawn.

For a few years now, people have been using AI to try. Recording hundreds of hours of calls, songs, squeaks. Finding patterns, repetitions, what looks like structure.

Result? We still don't understand a thing.

**Visual:** REEL — dog/cat staring into the lens → transition to JC wildlife portrait (heron, bird, mammal — a strong frame).

**Audio:** Soft pad, slow build. Voice calm, not "poetic."

---

## Scene 2 — The Wall (0:40 → 1:30)

**VO:**
Why doesn't it work? Because spotting a pattern is not the same as understanding meaning.

In 2026, researchers ran a simple test. They took baby cries — sounds we know well. A baby is hungry, tired, scared — we can tell. They asked AI to sort the cries by what they mean. Result: the AI grouped together cries of different meaning. And it separated cries of the same meaning. It saw the shape of the sound. Not what it meant.

It's the same with animals. We found patterns in sperm whale clicks. Rules, repetitions, structure. Nobody knows what they're saying. Elephants have individual names — we can't even point to where, in the sound, the name hides.

Patterns? We can find them. Meaning? Not yet.

**Visual:** MOTION — abstract spectrogram → REEL baby face (stock) → REEL whale/sperm whale → MOTION text overlay: "pattern ≠ meaning"

**Note:** the baby example is key — everyone understands a crying baby. It grounds the problem.

---

## Scene 3 — The Voynich Parallel (1:30 → 2:45)

**VO:**
There's a book that shows this exact problem. The Voynich manuscript. Six hundred years old. Plants that don't exist. A script nobody knows.

For twenty years, AI has been analyzing this book. The words aren't random. They follow rules — like a real language. And yet: nobody can read it.

Why? Because AI needs two things to decipher something. One: know the source language. Two: have lots of text in that language.

When it has both, it works well. You've probably heard about the Herculaneum scrolls. Papyrus carbonized by Vesuvius in 79 AD. Impossible to unroll. In 2026, for the first time, an entire scroll was read using AI. That's a real achievement. But it was Ancient Greek — a language we already know. The AI didn't translate meaning. It just made the ink visible.

Without a key, without a dictionary, without someone who knows the language, AI is blind. It's exactly the same for animals. The Voynich is an archaeology problem. Whale song is a biology problem. Same wall: the data is there. The key is missing.

**Visual:** REEL — Voynich page (slow zoom) → MOTION — two bullet points "1. know the language / 2. lots of text" → REEL — Herculaneum scroll (stock) → **KEY VISUAL MOMENT:** crossfade between a Voynich page and a whale spectrogram (MOTION) — two sides of the same wall

**Note:** the Voynich ↔ spectrogram crossfade must be PERFECT. It's the one shot that makes the parallel click in one second.

---

## Scene 4 — The Sensory Trap (2:45 → 3:30)

**VO:**
There's another problem, more subtle. We look at the world through our senses. But our senses are limited.

The bee sees ultraviolet patterns on flowers. We just see white. We hear from 20 to 20,000 hertz. The bat goes up to 120,000. The dolphin to 150,000. The elephant down to 5.

For decades, we missed entire sounds. Rodent ultrasounds. Elephant infrasounds. Why? Because our microphones stopped where our ears stop.

A biologist called this the "Umwelt" — the world as each species perceives it. Every animal lives in its own sensory bubble. We only see a small piece of it.

**Visual:** REEL — flower in visible light → transition to same flower in UV (or simulation). This is THE shot that makes Umwelt visible. → MOTION — frequency spectrum with animals positioned (20 Hz elephant → 20 kHz human → 150 kHz dolphin)

**Note:** the visible→UV transition is the strong moment. If no real footage, a clean MOTION simulation works.

---

## Scene 5 — So What Can We Do? (3:30 → 4:30)

**VO:**
So nothing can be done? Yes, something can. But we need to stop promising a magic translation — and start doing what actually works.

**One: pair sound and image.** In nature, a call is almost always linked to a behaviour. A bird sings, we see it. An animal alerts, we see why. If a camera films at the same time the microphone records, we can start connecting sounds to specific events. That's how you build a real key.

**Two: listen instead of impose.** For sixty years, we tried to teach our words to animals. Chimpanzees learning sign language, dolphins mimicking whistles. Impressive results. But look closely — it falls apart. No syntax, no new sentences. A lot of language, not real dialogue. Now we do the opposite: we listen to the sounds they already make. Patiently. In bulk. We switch from teacher to student.

**Three: stop selling dreams.** Apps that "translate" your dog? That's marketing. But detecting whether an animal is afraid, in pain, on alert — those first results are here, and they're already useful. For animal welfare. For conservation.

**Visual:** MOTION — three bullet points appearing one by one:
1. Sound + image → 🔑
2. Listen → 🎧
3. Useful → ✅
→ REEL — JC atmosphere photo (animal looking toward photographer)

**Note:** the tone turns concrete. No poetry — tangible, immediately understandable things.

---

## Scene 6 — Conclusion (4:30 → 5:00)

**VO:**
So no, we're probably not going to "translate" whale song tomorrow morning. Probably never.

But we can learn to listen better. And maybe that's the most important thing: building a connection, even without a dictionary. Even without understanding every word.

**Visual:** REEL — wide nature shot, sunset or mist, silence settling in → fade to black → WildLens logo

**Note:** the ending is open, humble, but positive. Not a wall — a door.

## VO Notes — Tone and Pace

- **Pace:** ~130 words/min (relaxed, not rushed)
- **Sentences:** short. One idea = one sentence
- **"You"** — direct address to the viewer
- **Questions:** ask them, then answer. Creates a dialogue
- **Avoid:** umwelt (keep the word but explain quickly), epistemological, mutualism, co-evolutionary
- **Prefer:** "the animal's world", "the key is missing", "we don't know"
- **Concessions:** yes (we don't have all the answers, but here's what we can do)
- **Humility:** yes (we're not promising translation, we're learning to listen)
