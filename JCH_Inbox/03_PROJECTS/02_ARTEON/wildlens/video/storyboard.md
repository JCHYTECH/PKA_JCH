# Storyboard — « AI Will Never Speak the Language of the Living »

> Video essay WildLens · ~8-10 min · voix off EN · YouTube.
> **Légende des plans** :
> - **REEL** = photos/vidéos réelles (photothèque JC ou stock Storyblocks/Artgrid).
> - **IA** = clip abstrait généré (Veo/Runway) — jamais de faune.
> - **MOTION** = diagramme / typographie animée (After Effects, Blender, ou Canva).

## Scene 1 — Cold open (0:00 → 0:40)
- **VO :** Every two years, the same headline: artificial intelligence has deciphered the Voynich manuscript. And every two years, it's not true. In 2018, two researchers claimed it was Hebrew in code. The world believed them for a week. Then the claim fell apart. Six hundred years old — and still, nobody can read it. But the Voynich is not the story. It's a symptom of a deeper limit. A limit that runs through ancient books, through whale song, through the question we have always asked: can we speak with the living — and at what cost?
- **Plan :** REEL — gros plan page Voynich (`03-manuscrit-voynich.jpg`) + titre animé Space Grotesk.
- **Note :** hook < 15 s. Musique : nappe douce, montée lente.

## Scene 2 — Ch.1 Le manuscrit (0:40 → 2:10)
- **VO :** The Voynich is a real book. Two hundred pages. Plants that don't exist. A script that belongs to no known language. For years, artificial intelligence has dissected it: the frequencies, the word patterns, the structure. And the results are strange — the words are not random. They follow rules close to a natural language. And yet: no translation. The Voynich is not alone. Linear A, Rongorongo, the Indus script — the whole club of undeciphered scripts has passed through the algorithms. Without result. And here is the rule: AI decodes only when it already knows the language and has a large corpus. The proof is Herculaneum. In 2026, an entire scroll, buried by Vesuvius in the year 79, was read for the first time — with X-rays and machine learning. A miracle. But those were Greek texts. A language we already read. The machine didn't translate. It made the ink legible.
- **Plan :** REEL — Voynich (zoom lent) → MOTION (carte « Linear A · Rongorongo · Indus ») → REEL stock papyrus d'Herculanum.
- **Note :** le MOTION « la règle » = un bandeau texte.

## Scene 3 — Ch.2 Le mur animal (2:10 → 3:40)
- **VO :** Now here is the same wall, on the side of animals. In 2024, researchers proposed a phonetic alphabet for sperm whales. The structure is there — repeated, measurable patterns. The meaning stays closed. It's the Voynich in acoustic form. Structure is cheap. Meaning is not. And more data doesn't save us. The CETI project has collected enormous amounts of whale sound — and still, no translation. Because what's missing is not data. It's a key. A verifiable link between a signal and what it points to. A study from Tel Aviv made this painfully clear. They gave AI the sounds of toddlers — sounds we humans understand. And the AI grouped together sounds with different meanings, and separated sounds with the same meaning. It saw patterns. Not significance.
- **Plan :** MOTION — spectrogramme cachalot (`04-cachalot-spectrogramme.png`) qui « pulse » au rythme de la VO → REEL stock baleine.
- **Note :** superposer le spectrogramme sur l'image baleine.

## Scene 4 — Ch.3 Le cerveau (3:40 → 4:50)
- **VO :** Maybe the brain will save us. In the zebra finch, researchers did something remarkable: they rebuilt the bird's song from its neurons alone. They even decoded its dreams — asleep, the bird sings melodies it never sings awake. But here is the catch. This is motor production. How the brain moves the muscles that make the sound. Not what the sound means. You can read the score perfectly — and still hear no music.
- **Plan :** REEL — passereau (`05-cerveau-passereau.jpg`) → MOTION tracé neuronal → partition.
- **Note :** la partition qui reste « muette » = le point.

## Scene 5 — Ch.4 L'umwelt (4:50 → 6:20)
- **VO :** And there is something deeper. We reason about the world with our senses — and our senses are narrow. A bee sees ultraviolet landing strips on a flower that, to us, is just white. We hear twenty hertz to twenty kilohertz. A bat reaches a hundred and twenty. A dolphin, a hundred and fifty. An elephant goes down to five. For decades, we missed entire channels — rodent ultrasound, elephant infrasound — simply because our instruments stopped where our ears stopped. The biologist Jakob von Uexküll had a name for this: the umwelt. The world as each species perceives it. And sound is only one axis. There is chemistry — pheromones, a continent we barely smell. Electricity — fish that speak in electric fields. Magnetism, vibration. Each is a wavelength we might forget. Like analyzing a flower in RGB, and never switching to ultraviolet.
- **Plan :** REEL — fleur UV (`06b-fleur-uv.jpg`, le « avant/après » visible→UV) → MOTION spectre de fréquences (20 Hz → 150 kHz) → REEL grenouille (`06-umwelt-grenouille.jpg`).
- **Note :** le passage visible→UV est LE moment visuel fort.

## Scene 6 — Ch.5 Déduire l'umwelt (6:20 → 7:30)
- **VO :** So can AI deduce an umwelt we don't yet know? Not directly. No sensor, no data, no inference. An AI cannot train on a signal that never touched an instrument. Bird magnetoreception was not discovered by analysis — it was discovered by experiment. But AI can do something subtler: it can point to what is missing. This is how we found exoplanets — not by seeing them, but by the anomaly. How we found dark matter — an unexplained residue. Faced with two organisms that synchronize with no known channel, an algorithm can detect the gap. That's not a discovery. It's a prediction. Characterizing the medium is still physics, and sensors. The loop is real: measure wide, detect the gap, hypothesize a channel, build the sensor — and begin again. AI is a link in the loop. Not the engine.
- **Plan :** IA — clip abstrait « trou dans l'explication » (particules / onde qui s'arrête net) → MOTION boucle en 4 nœuds.
- **Note :** ici l'IA est légitime (abstrait, pas de faune).

## Scene 7 — Ch.6 Les anciens (7:30 → 8:50)
- **VO :** This dream of understanding animals is not new. The Quran says Solomon was taught the language of the birds. Attar wrote the Conference of the Birds in the twelfth century. In China, Gongye Chang, the son-in-law of Confucius, was said to understand them. The yogis spoke of a power: the knowledge of the sounds of all beings. And there is a real version of this dream. In Mozambique, the Yao people call a wild bird with a precise cry — brrr-hm — and the bird leads them to honey. Scientists proved the bird recognizes that exact call: it triples the odds of being guided. This is not translation. It is mutualism. Two species sharing a signal, neither decoding the other. The traditions made the dream into myth. Nature made it practice.
- **Plan :** REEL — corvidé (`08-anciens-corvide.jpg`) → MOTION manuscrits (Salomon/Attar, icônes) → REEL stock indicateur + chasseur Yao + gros « ×3 ».
- **Note :** le « ×3 » en plein écran = respiration.

## Scene 8 — Ch.7 La loi du dialecte (8:50 → 10:10)
- **VO :** One last intuition — the most beautiful of all. Birds have dialects. Their song is learned, and it drifts from valley to valley. But their alarm calls are not learned. They are innate, and they are conserved. And in the face of a predator — the dialect disappears. Unrelated birds share the same high whistle, the seet, because a predator cannot locate it. This is Peter Marler's discovery, and it was reconfirmed in 2025: birds separated by continents and millions of years share a nearly identical alarm call. The law, in one sentence: dialect is a luxury of signals that are not life-or-death. The moment a signal becomes a matter of survival, selection freezes it — and fuses it across species.
- **Plan :** REEL — mésange (`09-dialecte-mesange.jpg`) → MOTION deux ondes qui divergent (chant) puis fusionnent (alarme) → citation « Dialect is the luxury of non-vital signals ».
- **Note :** la divergence/convergence des ondes = le climax visuel.

## Scene 9 — Conclusion (10:10 → 11:00)
- **VO :** Three lessons, which are one. First: AI is a pattern machine, not a meaning machine. It can map the Voynich, the whale's alphabet, the finch's dreams. It cannot cross from pattern to meaning without a shared world. Second: our own umwelt is a trap. We search for animal language with human ears and human categories. The bee's flower does not exist in our spectrum. Third: meaning is not decoded — it is shared. The Yao hunter and the honeyguide do not translate one another. Over generations, they learned to inhabit the same signal. The machine maps what is measurable. The meaning belongs to the one who stays still, listens — and is willing to be looked back at.
- **Plan :** REEL — héron au crépuscule (`11-cloture-brume.jpg`) en parallaxe lent + signature WildLens.
- **Note :** fondu au noir, musique qui se tait.
